Solving Two-Step Equations

Work through the lesson in four stages: engage with the idea, explore a solution, apply your learning, and reflect on your strategy.

1. Engage 2. Explore 3. Apply 4. Reflect

1 Engage

Start by thinking about what it means to keep an equation balanced. In the equation below, what is happening to \(x\)?

\[3x + 5 = 20\]

Think, then share:
  • What operations are being performed on \(x\)?
  • What could you do to undo those operations?
  • Why should the same operation be applied to both sides?
Try an idea on paper before moving on.

2 Explore

Watch the lesson and notice how inverse operations help isolate the variable. Pause the video to predict the next step before it is shown.

Open this video on YouTube

Explore a worked example

\[3x + 5 = 20\]

  1. Undo adding 5 by subtracting 5 from both sides.
    \[3x + 5 - 5 = 20 - 5\]
    \[3x = 15\]
  2. Undo multiplying by 3 by dividing both sides by 3.
    \[\frac{3x}{3} = \frac{15}{3}\]
    \[x = 5\]
  3. Check by substituting 5 for \(x\).
    \[3(5) + 5 = 20\]
    \[15 + 5 = 20\]

The solution is \(\boxed{x = 5}\).

Notice: We undo addition before undoing multiplication. Explain why this order gets \(x\) by itself.

3 Apply

Now solve five randomly generated two-step equations. Choose an answer to get feedback about your reasoning.

4 Reflect

Summarize your strategy in your own words. Record your thoughts in your notes or discuss them with a partner.

  1. How do you decide which operation to undo first?
  2. Why must you perform the same operation on both sides of an equation?
  3. How can substituting your answer help you check your work?
Self-check: Can you identify the operations, undo them in reverse order, and verify your solution? If one step still feels uncertain, revisit the worked example before trying another question set.
